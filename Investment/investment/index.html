    Section 0: Core Money Principles (Start Here)

    • Budgeting: Follow 50-30-20 Rule
      50% Needs (rent, food, bills) | 30% Wants (lifestyle) | 20% Savings + Debt repayment + Investing
    • Pay Yourself First: Automate savings & investments every month.
    • Clear High-Interest Debt before aggressive investing.
    • Set Clear Financial Goals (short, medium, long-term) and assess your Risk Tolerance.

    Section 1: Non-Negotiable Foundations

    1. Health Insurance – Comprehensive mediclaim (family floater)
    2. Term Life Insurance – 10–15× annual income (pure protection)
    3. Emergency Fund – 6–9 months of living expenses in highly liquid & safe options (savings / liquid funds / FD)

    Section 2: Investment Basics

    Bond vs Fixed Deposit (FD)

    Feature Bonds Fixed Deposit (FD)
    Risk Low to moderate Very low (up to ₹5 lakh insured)
    Return Slightly higher Lower
    Liquidity Secondary market (need buyer) Easy premature withdrawal
    Maturity Fixed Flexible

    Mutual Funds / ETFs: Market-linked risk. Best starter vehicles.

    Time Horizon Rule

    • >10 years → Equity heavy (ETFs/MFs low risk | Stocks high risk)
    • 5–10 years → Balanced portfolio
    • <5 years → Debt/Bonds or FD
    • <6 months → Savings / Liquid FD

    Section 3: Indian Stock Market Structure

    1. Cash Market (Equity Market)

    • Normal share market where most beginners start
    • What you trade: Actual shares of companies (TCS, Reliance, Infosys, HDFC, etc.)
    • Order Types:
    • CNC → Delivery (you own the shares)
    • MIS → Intraday (no ownership, must square off same day)

    2. Derivative Market (F&O)

    • You don’t buy shares, you trade contracts
    • Futures: Agreement to buy/sell at a fixed price on a future date
    • Options: Right (not obligation) to buy/sell
    • Call (CE) → Bullish view
    • Put (PE) → Bearish view

    Section 4: TRADING 101 – A Beginner’s Guide

    1. What is Trading?

    • Buying and selling financial instruments with the goal of making a profit from price movements.

    • Trading & Investing Options (Risk Pyramid)

    • ETFs (Safest for beginners) – Nifty 50, Bank Nifty, Gold ETF

    • Stock Trading
    • Intraday: Same-day buy & sell (high risk, needs screen time)
    • Swing Trading: Hold for days/weeks (Best for beginners – charts & indicators)
    • Positional: Hold for weeks/months
    • Futures & Options (F&O) – High risk & advanced
    • Options Buyer: Limited loss
    • Options Seller: Higher probability but unlimited risk (without hedge)
    • Commodity Trading (MCX) – Gold, Silver, Crude Oil, Natural Gas (volatile)
    • Forex – Only RBI-approved pairs (e.g. USD/INR). Avoid illegal offshore apps
    • Crypto – High volatility, 24/7 market, heavy taxes, regulatory risk

    • Algo Trading – Excellent future option (leverage your Python & programming skills)

    • Key Mindset: You don’t need to be smart. You need a plan and discipline.

    2. How the Market Works

    • Market = Place where buyers and sellers meet.
    • Price moves due to supply & demand.
    • More buyers → Demand > Supply → Price goes UP
    • More sellers → Supply > Demand → Price goes DOWN
    • Prices move in trends: Uptrend, Downtrend ,Sideways

    4. Key Terms

    Term Meaning
    Long (Buy) Expect price to go up
    Short (Sell) Expect price to go down
    Support Price level where buyers step in
    Resistance Price level where sellers step in
    Breakout Price breaks support/resistance
    Pullback Temporary retrace before trend continues
    Volatility How much price moves
    Liquidity How easily you can buy/sell without affecting price
    Spread Difference between bid and ask

    5. The Trading Process

    1. Analyze – Technical + Fundamental
    2. Plan Your Trade – Entry, stop loss, take profit, position size
    3. Execute – Follow the plan (no emotions)
    4. Manage the Trade – Adjust, move stops, take partial profits
    5. Review & Learn – Journal everything

    6. Risk Management (Most Important)

    • Risk only 1-2% of account per trade.
    • Always use a Stop Loss.
    • Use proper position sizing.
    • Survive first. Profitability comes second.

    7. Common Analysis Methods

    • Technical Analysis: Charts, patterns, indicators (Support/Resistance, Trend lines, Moving Averages, RSI, MACD)
    • Fundamental Analysis: Economic news, earnings, interest rates, geopolitics.

    8. Simple Strategies to Start

    • Trend Following: Trade in the direction of the trend.
    • Breakout Trading: Trade when price breaks key levels.

    Reality: 80–90% retail traders lose money. Paper trade + small capital only.

    Section 5: Trend Analysis

    Types of Trends

    1. Uptrend
    2. Higher highs and higher lows
    3. Join the bottoms to draw trendline

    4. Strategy: Buy at start, sell at end of trend

    5. Downtrend

    6. Lower highs and lower lows
    7. Join the peaks to draw trendline

    8. Strategy: Sell (short) at start, buy back at end (mainly in F&O)

    9. Sideways Trend

    10. Price moves in a range (no clear direction)

    Recommended Timeframes - Long-term Investment: Weekly chart (7–8 years data) - Positional Trading: Daily chart (2–4 years data) - Intraday Trading: 15-minute chart (6–8 months data)

    Key Tip: - Timeframes 1m, 5m, 15m, 1H, 4H, 1D, 1W - Always identify the trend on higher timeframe first (Daily/Weekly). - Higher timeframes = bigger picture. Lower timeframes = timing entries.

    Section 6: Trendlines, Support & Resistance

    Trendline Confirmation

    • Uptrend confirmed when 3rd point also touches the line joining bottoms
    • Downtrend confirmed when 3rd point also touches the line joining peaks

    Support & Resistance

    • Support: Price level where stock usually stops falling (buyers enter)
    • Resistance: Price level where stock usually stops rising (sellers enter)

    Why They Matter: - Best entry & exit points - Stop-loss placement - Avoid bad trades

    Practical Trade Logic (With Buffer)

    • Support = ₹98 | Resistance = ₹102
    Action Price Level Reason
    Buy ₹98.5 – ₹99 (Support + 0.5–1%) Price often breaks slightly
    Stop Loss ₹97.8 (Support – 0.2–0.5%) If broken strongly, idea is wrong
    Target ₹101.5 – ₹102 (Resistance – 0.5–1%) Sellers act before exact level

    Types of Support & Resistance

    • Static: Fixed levels (Fibonacci, round numbers, psychological levels, trendlines)
    • Dynamic: Change with price (Moving Averages, Price Channels)

    Section 8: Risk Management & Short Selling

    Short Selling

    • Selling a security you don’t own
    • Borrow from broker → Sell → Later buy back & return
    • In Equity (Cash): Must square off same day (MIS)
    • In F&O: Can hold overnight (Futures/Options)

    Trade Terminology: - Normal Buy → Buy → Exit - Short Sell → Sell → Exit

    Order Types & Risk Management

    • LIMIT Order: Execute at this price or better (may not execute)
    • STOP Order: Triggers at a specific price level
    • Stop Loss (Must Use): SL-Market (SL-M) for guaranteed exit, Trailing Stop Loss to lock profits
    • Key Rules: MIS → CNC conversion allowed (extra STT + DP charges)
    • Upper Circuit = Only buyers | Lower Circuit = Only sellers (difficult to exit)

    Golden Rules: - Protect capital first - Use Stop Loss in every trade - Never make loss-to-delivery conversion a habit - Delivery trading is not for daily income (Swing 2–10 days works best) - Discipline > Strategy

    Section 9: How to Analyze Stocks (Fundamental Analysis)

    Types of Stock Analysis

    1. Fundamental Analysis - Evaluates company’s business, financials, management, and future potential - Focuses on Intrinsic Value - Uses: Profit & Loss Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement - Best for long-term investment

    Key Takeaways: - Look at company fundamentals and growth - Compare with peers - Ideal for long-term wealth creation

    2. Technical Analysis - Studies price trends and patterns - Based on supply and demand - Best for short to medium-term trading - Uses charts and indicators (EMA, RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, etc.)

    Section 10: Key Financial Ratios for Stock Valuation

    Ratio Formula Purpose
    P/E Ratio Share Price / EPS How much investors pay for each rupee of earnings
    P/B Ratio Share Price / Book Value per Share Compares market price to net assets (below 1 = possibly undervalued)
    PEG Ratio P/E Ratio / Earnings Growth Rate Adjusts P/E for growth (ideal near 1)
    Dividend Yield Annual Dividend / Share Price Return from dividends
    Dividend Payout Ratio Dividends / EPS % of earnings paid as dividends
    ROE Net Income / Shareholders’ Equity How efficiently company uses equity (higher is better)
    ROA Net Income / Total Assets Efficiency in using assets
    Debt-to-Equity Ratio Total Liabilities / Shareholders’ Equity Financial risk (lower is safer)
    Current Ratio Current Assets / Current Liabilities Short-term liquidity (>1 is good)
    Quick Ratio (Current Assets – Inventory) / Current Liabilities Stricter liquidity test
    Free Cash Flow (FCF) Operating Cash Flow – Capital Expenditure Cash available after investments

    Section 11: Intrinsic Value vs Market Value

    • Intrinsic Value: True worth based on fundamentals (DCF, P/E method, Asset-based)
    • Market Value: Current trading price

    Decision Rule: - Intrinsic Value > Market Value → Undervalued → Good to BUY - Intrinsic Value < Market Value → Overvalued → Consider Selling

    Ways to Calculate Intrinsic Value

    1. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF): Most accurate but complex
    2. P/E Method: Intrinsic Value = EPS × (1 + r) × P/E Ratio
    3. Asset-based Valuation: Assets – Liabilities (from balance sheet)

    Section 12: Fundamental Insight – Return on Equity (ROE)

    • ROE = Net Income / Shareholders’ Equity
    • Higher ROE = Better company performance
    • Shareholders’ Equity = Total Assets – Total Debt
    • Net Income = Revenue – All expenses (including interest & taxes)
